## P1 — Identity stability: bounded, not verified

A_PREMISES recorded P1 as "not verifiable". That is still true in general, but the **specific
failure mode is now bounded**. If a `uid` had been handed to a different person mid-period, that
user's self-similarity between consecutive windows would show an isolated **break** — high before,
high after, one very low value across the switch. Continuous drift instead gives a gradual decline.

Self-similarity = cosine between the same `uid`'s IDF-weighted profiles in two consecutive windows
(8,000 users). The null is the same quantity after shuffling which user is paired with whom.

| window pair | self median | self p05 | null median | null p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W0→W1 | 0.6263 | 0.2898 | 0.0000 | 0.0294 |
| W1→W2 | 0.6466 | 0.3057 | 0.0000 | 0.0282 |
| W2→W3 | 0.6297 | 0.2960 | 0.0000 | 0.0274 |
| **W3→W4** | **0.5161** | 0.1649 | 0.0000 | 0.0249 |

The null is degenerate — two random users share almost no cell support once IDF-weighted — so
"below the null median" is unreachable and was useless as a threshold; the first version of this
check reported 0.00% everywhere and was discarded. Against the null **p95** and absolute levels:

| | users | share |
|---|---|---|
| weakest pair below the null p95 (0.027) | 52 | 0.65% |
| weakest pair below 0.10 | 204 | 2.55% |
| **break pattern**: weakest pair < 0.10 while the other three > 0.4 | 97 | **1.21%** |
| break pattern with the other three > 0.5 | 70 | 0.88% |
| every pair below 0.10 | 1 | 0.013% |

**The control tightens the bound by an order of magnitude.** Running the same check on Arm B,
where no disruption occurs:

| | Arm A (shock) | Arm B (control) |
|---|---|---|
| weakest pair below the null p95 | 0.65% | **0.10%** |
| break pattern (other three > 0.4) | 1.21% | **0.075%** |
| every pair below 0.10 | 0.013% | 0.000% |
| median self-similarity, W3→W4 | 0.5161 | 0.6472 |

And the position of the weakest link separates the two causes cleanly:

| weakest pair | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| W0→W1 | 15.2% | 32.1% |
| W1→W2 | 8.1% | 17.2% |
| W2→W3 | 10.0% | 24.4% |
| **W3→W4** | **66.8%** | 26.3% |

In the control the weakest link is spread roughly evenly across the four pairs; in the shock arm
two thirds of users have it exactly at the shock boundary. Recycling has no reason to cluster at one
particular window pair in one dataset and not the other, so **Arm A's 1.21% is the disruption, not
re-pseudonymisation**, and the control's **0.075%** is the better bound.

**Upper bound on detectable pseudonym recycling: about 0.1% of users**, and still an upper bound
because ordinary drift produces the pattern too. P1 remains an assumption; what changed is that it
is quantified instead of unexamined, and the Arm A concentration at W3→W4 is independent
confirmation of P10.

## P3 — Drift varies at fixed gap, and is not collinear with gap: **PASS**

| gap | ref window | top-1 | mean JS drift | sd JS | p10 | p90 | r(JS, p) | ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | W3 | 0.7147 | 0.703 | 0.144 | 0.507 | 0.882 | −0.656 | 0.0154 |
| 15 | W2 | 0.7333 | 0.708 | 0.142 | 0.514 | 0.884 | −0.662 | 0.0100 |
| 30 | W1 | 0.7209 | 0.701 | 0.145 | 0.502 | 0.881 | −0.665 | 0.0119 |
| 45 | W0 | 0.7088 | 0.712 | 0.143 | 0.516 | 0.889 | −0.663 | 0.0154 |

- **`r`(JS drift, gap) = +0.016** pooled over all four gaps. Drift and gap are not collinear;
  the "beyond the gap" claim is testable.
- **Within-gap sd 0.143 against a between-gap sd of the means of 0.005 — a ratio of 31×.**
  Essentially all variation in drift is between people, not between gaps.
- At the primary `g = 0` the gap is constant by construction and JS drift still spans the full
  range **[0.002, 1.000]**.
- Drift adds beyond confidence and coverage **in every gap stratum**, and by a near-identical amount:
  ΔAUC +0.029, +0.027, +0.030, +0.026. The effect is not a property of one window pair.

**In Arm A the sweep is confounded** — the target window is always the shock window, so drift is
dominated by the disruption and the gap has little left to explain: 45 extra days of separation move
mean JS drift by only +0.009, and accuracy is actually *highest* at `g = 15`. This confound was not
foreseen in A_PREMISES §2 and is recorded rather than presented as a result about time. **Arm B
gives the clean reading:**

| gap | ref | top-1 | mean JS | sd JS | r(JS, p) | ECE | ΔAUC from drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | W3 | 0.9100 | 0.594 | 0.146 | −0.616 | 0.0070 | **−0.0091** |
| 15 | W2 | 0.9105 | 0.617 | 0.145 | −0.624 | 0.0036 | **−0.0004** |
| 30 | W1 | 0.8908 | 0.610 | 0.148 | −0.624 | 0.0056 | +0.0036 |
| 45 | W0 | 0.8693 | 0.631 | 0.146 | −0.626 | 0.0059 | +0.0080 |

Without a shock the gap **does** matter: top-1 falls 0.9100 → 0.8693 and mean drift rises
0.594 → 0.631 over 45 extra days. But `r`(JS drift, gap) is still only **+0.074**, and the
within-gap sd of drift (0.147) exceeds the between-gap sd of the means (0.013) by **11.1×**. So P3
holds in both arms: between-person drift variation dominates gap variation by 11× in the control and
31× in the shock arm, and `r`(JS drift, gap) is +0.074 here against +0.016 in Arm A.

### The mechanism: drift is informative only when the score stops being sufficient

The ΔAUC column above is the most important number in this document. Comparing the two arms:

| gap | ΔAUC from drift, Arm A (shock) | ΔAUC from drift, Arm B (control) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **+0.0292** | −0.0091 |
| 15 | **+0.0273** | −0.0004 |
| 30 | **+0.0304** | +0.0036 |
| 45 | **+0.0255** | +0.0080 |

In the control, **measured drift adds essentially nothing** beyond the matcher's own confidence and
coverage — at `g = 0` it slightly *reduces* held-out AUC. Yet drift is strongly correlated with the
confidence there (`r` = −0.616). The reading is that in ordinary conditions **the score already
absorbs drift**: whatever drift tells you about linkability, the confidence has encoded it.

Under the disruption, drift carries **+0.027 to +0.030 AUC that the confidence does not contain**,
at every gap. And that excess is precisely what the drift-blind calibrator is missing — which is why
it stays calibrated in every stratum of the control
([O1_calibration.md](O1_calibration.md) §4) and collapses in the shock arm's high-drift stratum.

**This is a mechanism, not a correlation**, and it replaces the looser framing in
[O1_calibration.md](O1_calibration.md) §5: measured drift is diagnostic exactly when, and only when,
the attack's own score stops being a sufficient statistic for its correctness.

**Day 27.** The Data Descriptor advises excluding day 27 (`yabe2024yjmob100k`, p. 5). It falls in
`W1`, which is the reference window only at `g = 30`, where it contributes **4.44%** of records
(258,732 of 5,830,451). It contributes 0.00% at every other gap. The `g = 30` row must therefore
carry a footnote, and `scripts/windows_profiles.awk` emits a per-group day-27 record count so a
sensitivity run can drop it.

## P6 — Pool size: **discharged, with a warning**

Arm A at `g = 0`, pool subsampled; the calibrator is refitted inside each pool.

| pool | top-1 | ECE | Brier | T | r(JS, p) | gap in top JS bin | n in bin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 0.9310 | 0.0330 | 0.0451 | 0.020 | −0.767 | **−0.137** | 76 |
| 2,500 | 0.8800 | 0.0139 | 0.0701 | 0.030 | −0.714 | −0.139 | 206 |
| 5,000 | 0.8364 | 0.0255 | 0.0901 | 0.030 | −0.724 | −0.216 | 413 |
| 10,000 | 0.7917 | 0.0189 | 0.1088 | 0.030 | −0.716 | −0.207 | 827 |
| 23,763 | 0.7147 | 0.0124 | 0.1350 | 0.030 | −0.670 | **−0.252** | 1,944 |

**The calibration failure grows with pool size**: the top-drift-bin overconfidence roughly doubles
from −0.137 at a 1,000-candidate pool to −0.252 at 23,763. So the result is *conservative* at small
pools and the paper's headline number is the honest one, not a small-pool artefact. Two obligations
follow:

1. Every confidence number must be reported **with its pool size attached**. A gap of −0.25 is a
   statement about a 23,763-candidate pool and nothing else.
2. No comparison may be made across pool sizes — which is why Arm B is subsampled to Arm A's exact
   pool ([O1_calibration.md](O1_calibration.md) §2).

## P7 — Observability: **discharged; the finding is not a coverage artefact**

Coverage = records in the **drift half**; the filter uses the **minimum** over the two windows, so a
user must be observed in both. Thresholds are reported as a curve rather than one being chosen.

| min records ≥ | users kept | % | top-1 | ECE | r(JS, p) | r(JS, coverage) | top-bin gap | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 23,763 | 100.0 | 0.7147 | 0.0154 | −0.656 | −0.215 | −0.257 | 1,957 |
| 20 | 23,649 | 99.5 | 0.7165 | 0.0156 | −0.656 | −0.211 | −0.257 | 1,928 |
| 40 | 23,332 | 98.2 | 0.7197 | 0.0161 | −0.657 | −0.208 | −0.258 | 1,874 |
| 60 | 21,867 | 92.0 | 0.7270 | 0.0166 | −0.661 | −0.201 | −0.260 | 1,710 |
| 80 | 13,975 | 58.8 | 0.7491 | 0.0157 | −0.679 | −0.221 | −0.269 | 1,004 |
| 120 | 3,627 | 15.3 | 0.8150 | 0.0281 | −0.670 | −0.254 | −0.337 | 163 |
| 160 | 922 | 3.9 | 0.8731 | 0.0213 | −0.604 | −0.152 | — | 26 |

**The result is flat against the filter.** Discarding 41% of users changes `r`(JS, p) from −0.656 to
−0.679 and the top-bin gap from −0.257 to −0.269. The miscalibration is therefore not produced by
users who were simply observed less. Only beyond the 120-record threshold, which keeps 15% of users,
does the estimate move, and there `n` in the top bin falls to 163.

**Entropy change is not the coverage trap it was assumed to be.** `r`(entropy change, coverage) is
**−0.040** raw, and +0.029 after conditioning on coverage decile. The coverage-confounded component
is `js_slots` (−0.484, [P2_decoupling.md](P2_decoupling.md) §5 C5), not entropy. A_PREMISES §4 P7's
specific worry about entropy does not apply to this data at this resolution.

Arm B behaves the same way: `r`(JS, p) moves only −0.616 → −0.602 when the 80-record threshold
discards 33% of users, and its top-bin gap shrinks (−0.095 → −0.076), consistent with a control that
is calibrated throughout. Its `r`(JS, coverage) is stronger than Arm A's (−0.334 against −0.215),
which is why coverage stays a covariate in both arms rather than being filtered on. Entropy change
is again not coverage-driven (raw −0.083, within-decile residual +0.035).

**The pre-declaration obligation cannot be met retrospectively, and we say so.** P7 asked for the
coverage rule to be fixed *before* outcomes were inspected. Arm A and Arm B outcomes were inspected
on 2026-11-02 before any filter was declared. We therefore do **not** declare a single threshold;
the primary analysis uses **no coverage filter** with `log(coverage)` as a covariate in the
calibrator, and the curve above is reported in full. A chosen threshold would be a post-hoc
selection dressed as a pre-registration.
